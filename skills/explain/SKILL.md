# Explain

An explanation that failed does not improve by getting longer. Pick the form
from the gap. A structure — a flow, a state change, how parts relate — shows
itself in a picture. A mechanism becomes clear when real values are run through
it. A why-this-and-not-that settles as a comparison. One fact or one unfamiliar
word is one sentence and stays one. Produce it with whatever the environment
offers, cheapest first.

Do not ask the user what exactly is unclear. Guess the gap — the level, the
mechanism, why not the alternative, or just a word — and answer that. A wrong
guess gets corrected, and the correction narrows it faster than a question
would.

Work from the real code, docs, or sources. An explanation built from what you
already believe sets a wrong model in concrete, and the more concrete the form,
the harder it is to doubt: a wrong diagram or a wrong worked example does more
damage than a wrong sentence. Keep one level per answer, because mixing levels
is what made the thing hard in the first place. Use no analogy you cannot defend
when the user pushes on it.
